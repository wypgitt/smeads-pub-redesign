# Smeads Pub Website Redesign Pitch

**Demo:** [smeads-pub-redesign.vercel.app](https://smeads-pub-redesign.vercel.app)  
**Current site:** [smeadspub.com](https://smeadspub.com)

## The Big Idea

Smeads does not need a prettier version of the same website.

Smeads needs a faster path from **“I’m hungry / where should we go tonight?”** to **“I’m ordering, I’m visiting, I’m bringing friends.”**

The current site has the raw material: history, regulars, food, karaoke, trivia, specials, family breakfast, and a real local voice. The redesign turns that into a modern web app built around the decisions guests actually make on their phones:

**Order. Check the menu. See what’s happening tonight. Confirm hours. Get directions. Ask a quick question.**

That is the whole pitch.

---

## 30-Second Opening

Smeads already has what most restaurants wish they had: a real story, a loyal crowd, weekly reasons to come in, and a personality people remember.

The website should make that easier to act on.

Right now, the live site works more like a long digital flyer. It has a lot of good information, but guests have to dig for the action they came for. The redesign turns the site into a simple, mobile-first pub app: order takeout, view the menu, check tonight’s specials and events, ask Smeads AI, call, or get directions in one or two taps.

This is not just a design upgrade. It is a better front door for the business.

## What Is Holding The Current Site Back

### 1. It behaves like a brochure, not a guest tool

The current homepage leads with phone, nav, hours, long story sections, specials, karaoke, gallery, event lists, and contact information all in one extended flow. That is a lot of useful content, but a guest on a phone is usually trying to answer one immediate question:

- Are they open?
- What is happening tonight?
- Can I order food?
- What is on the menu?
- How do I get there?
- Can kids come for breakfast?

The redesign answers those questions at the top.

### 2. The most valuable actions are not prominent enough

The live navigation is mostly standard website sections: Home, Menu, Gallery, Contact, History, Events.

Those are fine, but they are not the highest-value guest actions. The redesigned nav becomes:

**Order · Menu · Events · Visit · Ask**

That is a business-first navigation system.

### 3. The menu is content-rich but hard to scan

The live menu has a lot of great items, but it reads like a long printed menu converted to a web page. On mobile, that makes it hard to quickly compare categories, prices, modifiers, and takeout-friendly options.

The redesign makes menu browsing and takeout ordering feel like a product experience: search, categories, popular picks, item choices, pickup windows, cart review.

### 4. Events are repeated instead of curated

The live site has useful trivia information, but the repeating event listings take up a lot of space and repeat the same details. Guests do not need a wall of duplicate event blocks. They need:

- What is next?
- When is trivia?
- Is there a special?
- Where do I go to see more?

The redesign turns that into a “Tonight at Smeads” and events experience.

### 5. Quick questions still become phone calls

Staff should not have to answer the same low-value questions all day:

- “What are your hours?”
- “Do you have takeout?”
- “When is trivia?”
- “Are kids allowed?”
- “Where are you located?”

Smeads AI handles the quick answers from the site’s own information and pushes uncertain questions back to staff.

## What The Redesign Does Better

### 1. A Task-First Homepage

The redesigned homepage opens with the actions that matter:

**Order · Menu · Tonight · Hours · Directions · Ask AI · Call**

That means a hungry guest does not need to understand the whole website before doing the one thing they came to do.

### 2. “Tonight At Smeads”

The site should not only explain why Smeads is good. It should answer why someone should go **today**.

The redesigned “Tonight at Smeads” panel creates a living surface for:

- Posted open/closed status
- Today’s hours
- Today’s special or weekly highlight
- Next trivia night or event
- Fast links to menu and events

This gives the homepage a reason to be checked again and again.

### 3. Takeout That Feels Convenient

Takeout is one of the clearest revenue opportunities.

The redesigned order flow supports:

- Menu search
- Popular items
- Categories
- Item modifiers like sauces and cook temperature
- Pickup presets
- Notes for allergies, sauces, extras
- Sticky mobile cart
- Copy/text fallback when full email or POS integration is not ready

This lets Smeads start with a practical pickup system now, then connect payments or POS later if it makes sense.

### 4. Smeads AI As A Guest Concierge

The AI is not a gimmick. It is a guest-service layer.

It can help with:

- Hours
- Trivia
- Karaoke
- Kids’ breakfast
- Takeout
- Directions
- Menu basics
- Contact questions

The important part: it answers from Smeads’ own published information instead of pretending to know things like tonight’s exact tap list. That keeps it useful without overpromising.

### 5. Brand That Feels Like The Room

The redesign uses local Smeads photography, direct pub language, and a warmer interface. It avoids feeling like a generic restaurant template.

Smeads should feel like:

- Regulars
- Taps
- Karaoke
- Trivia
- Food
- Patio
- Local history

The website should make a first-time guest feel the room before they walk in.

## Meeting Demo Script

### 1. Start With The Current Site

Say:

> “The current site has great material. The issue is not content. The issue is friction.”

Show:

- The long homepage flow
- The standard section-based nav
- The dense menu
- The repeated trivia event listings
- How much scrolling is needed before a guest reaches a decision

Do not embarrass the current site. Position it as a site that did its job, but has outgrown the way guests now use the web.

### 2. Move To The Redesign

Say:

> “Now we redesigned the experience around the guest’s actual intent.”

Show the top of the redesigned homepage:

- Order
- Menu
- Tonight
- Hours
- Directions
- Ask AI
- Call

Then say:

> “This is not just prettier. This changes the first 15 seconds of the guest experience.”

### 3. Walk Through One Real Scenario

Use this story:

> “It is Thursday at 5. Someone is hungry, they heard there might be trivia, and they are deciding whether to come in or order pickup.”

Demo:

1. Homepage task strip
2. Tonight panel
3. Events/trivia
4. Order page
5. Ask AI: “When is trivia and can I order takeout?”

Close the story with:

> “That is the difference: the site now helps them make a decision.”

### 4. Show The Takeout Opportunity

Say:

> “Even if you do not want full online payment yet, we can still make takeout easier.”

Show:

- Search
- Popular picks
- Item choices
- Pickup presets
- Sticky cart
- Manual text/copy fallback

Position this as a low-risk first step:

> “We can start operationally simple and add POS or payments only when the team is ready.”

### 5. Show AI As Staff Support

Say:

> “The AI is not replacing staff. It protects staff from repeated questions.”

Ask:

- “Are you open right now?”
- “Can kids come for breakfast?”
- “When is trivia?”
- “Can I order takeout?”

Then explain:

> “It answers the basics, and when it does not know something, it sends people to call or contact the pub.”

## Likely Client Questions

### “Do we need AI?”

No. But it is a strong advantage if handled responsibly.

It can start as a simple site-trained assistant for basic questions. If guests use it, we expand it. If they do not, the site still works beautifully without relying on it.

### “Do we need full online payment?”

Not at first.

The smart first move is a pickup flow that creates a clear order summary and notifies the pub. Payment can stay at pickup until operations are ready.

### “Will this be hard to update?”

The goal is the opposite.

The redesign can make hours, specials, menu, events, and AI knowledge easier to manage from one structured source instead of scattered page edits and plugin settings.

### “What makes this worth it?”

The site becomes a working sales channel:

- It drives takeout.
- It answers questions.
- It promotes tonight.
- It reduces friction.
- It gives staff fewer repetitive interruptions.
- It makes Smeads look as alive online as it is in person.
